# SEAT Assessment Scoring Rules

## How to score

Each of the 21 questions maps to one of four pillars: Strategy, Engagement, Assessment, or Training. Each answer is a maturity value from 1 to 5.

### Per-pillar score

Average the maturity values of all questions in that pillar.

| Pillar | Questions | Count |
|--------|-----------|-------|
| Strategy | Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4, Q6, Q7 | 6 |
| Engagement | Q9, Q10, Q11, Q12, Q13 | 5 |
| Assessment | Q5, Q8, Q14, Q15, Q16 | 5 |
| Training | Q17, Q18, Q19, Q20, Q21 | 5 |

### Overall maturity score

Average all four pillar scores. (This weights each pillar equally in the LLM-administered version. The hosted version at app.humanrisk.com applies weighted scoring: Strategy 30%, Engagement 25%, Assessment 25%, Training 20%.)

### Maturity levels

Map pillar averages and the overall average to a maturity level:

| Score range | Level | Description |
|-------------|-------|-------------|
| 1.0 -- 1.49 | **Reactive** | No implementation, awareness, or effort in this area. |
| 1.5 -- 2.49 | **Developing** | Some awareness or activity exists, but it is inconsistent or informal. |
| 2.5 -- 3.49 | **Defined** | The focus is addressed in a structured way but lacks sophistication or optimization. |
| 3.5 -- 4.49 | **Integrated** | A mature, well-integrated approach that is consistent and effective. |
| 4.5 -- 5.0 | **Embedded** | Exemplifies industry-leading practices, fully optimized for impact and efficiency. |

## Maturity level descriptions by pillar

### Reactive (1)

- **Strategy:** No documented security awareness strategy exists. Program lacks executive sponsorship, dedicated budget, or alignment with business objectives. Security awareness activities happen reactively without planning.
- **Engagement:** Employees receive minimal security communications, typically only during incidents. No targeted messaging for different roles or departments. Participation in security activities is entirely voluntary with low uptake.
- **Assessment:** No metrics are collected to measure program effectiveness. Risk assessments do not inform awareness priorities. Program impact on security posture is unknown and unmeasured.
- **Training:** Security training is either non-existent or delivered sporadically without structure. Content is generic, outdated, and not tailored to organizational risks or employee roles.

### Developing (2)

- **Strategy:** Basic awareness activities exist but lack formal documentation or strategic direction. Limited executive awareness of program value. Budget allocation is minimal and inconsistent year-over-year.
- **Engagement:** Occasional security newsletters or emails are sent organization-wide. Employee feedback mechanisms are limited. Security events like awareness month occur but lack sustained follow-up throughout the year.
- **Assessment:** Basic metrics like training completion rates are tracked sporadically. Phishing simulations may occur but results are not systematically analyzed. Program improvements are based on anecdotal feedback rather than data.
- **Training:** Annual security training exists but uses generic, off-the-shelf content. Training is primarily compliance-driven rather than risk-focused. No differentiation between roles or departments in training content.

### Defined (3)

- **Strategy:** Written security awareness strategy exists with defined goals and objectives. Program has dedicated owner but may lack sufficient resources. Strategy is reviewed annually and shows some alignment with business risk priorities.
- **Engagement:** Regular communication cadence established with quarterly security updates. Some role-based messaging for high-risk positions. Employee security champions program exists but participation is limited to volunteers.
- **Assessment:** Structured metrics program tracks key indicators like training completion, phishing click rates, and incident reporting. Quarterly program reviews occur but may lack comprehensive analysis of trends and root causes.
- **Training:** Structured annual training program with some customization for different risk levels. Content addresses current threat landscape. Training includes basic simulations but follow-up coaching is inconsistent.

### Integrated (4)

- **Strategy:** Comprehensive strategy document with measurable objectives tied to business outcomes. Strong executive sponsorship with dedicated budget and resources. Program strategy is integrated with broader risk management and updated based on threat intelligence.
- **Engagement:** Multi-channel communication strategy reaches all employee segments with targeted messaging. Active security champion network with formal recognition programs. Regular pulse surveys measure security culture and employee sentiment.
- **Assessment:** Comprehensive metrics dashboard tracks leading and lagging indicators across all program areas. Regular benchmarking against industry standards. Data-driven decision making with quarterly business reviews demonstrating program ROI.
- **Training:** Role-based training curriculum with specialized content for different risk profiles. Multiple delivery formats accommodate diverse learning preferences. Regular skills assessments validate knowledge retention and identify gaps.

### Embedded (5)

- **Strategy:** Industry-leading strategy that serves as a model for peer organizations. Fully integrated into business planning cycles with board-level visibility. Program demonstrates clear ROI and competitive advantage through superior security culture.
- **Engagement:** Security awareness is embedded in organizational culture with high employee engagement scores. Peer-to-peer learning networks and gamification drive sustained participation. Employees proactively report threats and share security insights.
- **Assessment:** Advanced analytics platform provides predictive insights and real-time risk indicators. Continuous improvement process with automated feedback loops. Program effectiveness is validated through third-party assessments and industry recognition.
- **Training:** Innovative, adaptive training platform that personalizes content based on individual risk profiles and learning patterns. Microlearning integrated into daily workflows. Training effectiveness measured through behavioral change and reduced security incidents.

## Interpreting your results

After scoring, identify:

1. **Your lowest-scoring pillar** -- this is where investment will have the highest impact.
2. **Any pillar more than 1 level below the others** -- this gap creates systemic risk regardless of strength elsewhere.
3. **The spread between your highest and lowest pillar** -- a spread greater than 1.5 points suggests the program is lopsided and would benefit from rebalancing.
