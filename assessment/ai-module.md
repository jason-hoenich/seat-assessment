# SEAT AI Module -- 7 Questions

Asked after the 21 core questions, one at a time, with all five options. Answers produce a separate **AI readiness** score and feed the `ai-risk-readiness` outcome. They never change pillar or overall scores, so results stay comparable with assessments taken before the module existed.

The same rules apply as for the core: if the respondent is between two levels, record the lower one, and N/A (0) is skipped in scoring.

### Q901: AI Use Policy and Ownership (Strategy)

How clearly does your organization define acceptable AI use, and who owns it?

*Covers generative AI, AI-assisted coding and AI agents. Ownership means a named person or team accountable for the policy, not just a document.*

1. No AI use policy and no owner
2. Informal guidance or a general policy line, with no clear owner
3. Written AI use policy with a named owner; the awareness program is consulted
4. Policy co-owned with security awareness and reviewed on a set schedule
5. Policy co-owned by security awareness, reviewed with leadership, and updated as tools change

### Q902: AI Tool Visibility (Strategy)

How well does your program know which AI tools employees use, sanctioned and unsanctioned?

*Includes free and personal accounts used for work. Visibility usually comes from DLP, CASB, browser or network telemetry, owned by another team.*

1. No visibility into which AI tools are used
2. Anecdotal knowledge from requests and incidents only
3. Known for sanctioned tools, partial for unsanctioned ones
4. Regular reporting across sanctioned and unsanctioned tools, by department
5. Continuous visibility across tools and account types, feeding program priorities

### Q903: AI Guidance Reach (Engagement)

Does your AI guidance reach the groups that use AI most, not just the enterprise tenant?

*Heavy AI use tends to concentrate in a few functions (often legal, sales and marketing, and engineering), and much of it happens outside the sanctioned tool.*

1. No AI guidance, or none beyond the policy itself
2. One all-staff message or module for everyone
3. Guidance targeted to some high-use groups
4. Role-specific guidance for most high-use groups
5. Tailored by role for every high-use group, including personal-account use

### Q904: AI-Enabled Social Engineering (Training)

How well are employees prepared for AI-enabled social engineering, such as deepfake voice and video or AI-written lures?

*Preparation means a verification habit (for example, calling back on a known number) that employees practice, not only awareness that deepfakes exist.*

1. Not covered
2. Mentioned in general awareness content
3. Covered in content, with a clear verification rule
4. Practiced in simulations for high-risk roles
5. Practiced in simulations, with out-of-band verification habits measured

### Q905: Safe AI Use (Training)

How well are employees trained in safe AI use: what to paste, reviewing output, and granting agent and tool permissions?

*Covers generative AI, AI-assisted coding and AI agents that act on an employee's behalf.*

1. Not covered
2. Policy acknowledgment only
3. Annual module on safe AI use
4. Role-specific training covering generative AI and AI-assisted coding
5. Role-specific and in the workflow, covering generative AI, AI-assisted coding and agents

### Q906: AI Behavior Measurement (Assessment)

How does your program measure AI-related behavior?

*Observed behavior means events such as sensitive data pasted into AI tools or permissions granted to AI apps, not completion or acknowledgment rates.*

1. Not measured
2. Policy acknowledgment and training completion only
3. Some observed behavior, reviewed occasionally
4. Observed behavior tracked regularly and reported
5. Observed behavior tracked, trended and used to set program priorities

### Q907: AI Incident Reporting (Assessment)

Do employees know how to report AI misuse or an AI mistake, and can the organization investigate it?

*For example, an employee who pasted customer data into a public AI tool: would they know where to report it, and would someone pick it up?*

1. No reporting path for AI misuse
2. Reported informally, if at all
3. A reporting path exists but is rarely used
4. A known reporting path that is used, with defined triage
5. Used regularly, and investigations feed back into training
