# ISO 27001

Mapping of SEAT outcomes to ISO/IEC 27001:2022 requirements for Information Security Management Systems (ISMS).

**Version:** 2022  
**Adapter last updated:** 2025-01-27  
**Official reference:** https://www.iso.org/standard/27001  
**Adapter id:** `iso27001`

## How an assistant applies this adapter

You need the respondent's per-question scores (1-5) from `assessment/questions.md`, produced by the flow in `AGENTS.md`. Then:

1. **Compute each outcome score.** Every outcome is scored from the questions listed for it in `adapters/outcomes.md`. Average those question scores, skipping any answered N/A, and round to one decimal.
2. **Decide met or gap, per assurance level.** The threshold depends on the row's assurance level, not a single global number:
   - Required: outcome score >= 4.5
   - Expected: outcome score >= 2.5
   - Recommended: outcome score >= 1.5
   At or above the row's threshold is **met**. Below it is a **gap**. There is no partial state; the platform scores this as met or not met, and this export matches it.
3. **Rows marked "Not assessed".** Some requirements map to a SEAT outcome that no core question feeds. Report these as **not assessed**, never as a gap, and leave them out of the overall score entirely. A gap means the program was measured and fell short. Not assessed means the 21-question core instrument does not cover it. Say so plainly, list the evidence the framework expects so the respondent knows what it would take, and note that the hosted assessment at https://app.humanrisk.com asks the additional framework-specific questions that score these outcomes.
4. **List missing evidence.** For any row that is a gap at Required or Expected assurance, report that row's evidence types as missing evidence together with its framework reference.
5. **Compute the overall alignment score.** Using only the scored rows: the effective score is the outcome score when met, and `(outcome score / 5) * threshold * 0.5` when it is a gap, which is the penalty the platform applies. Multiply each effective score by the row weight, sum, and divide by the total weight of the scored rows. Round to one decimal. The result is on a 0 to 5 scale, not a percentage.

Report the overall score, met/gap per requirement, any not-assessed requirements, and missing evidence for each gap. Do not invent requirements that are not in the table below, and never answer the assessment questions on the respondent's behalf.

## Mapping notes

**Strategic Alignment (Clause 6.1.2, A.5.10, A.5.22).** The ISMS must align with the organization's context, risk appetite, and strategic direction through risk assessment and information security planning.

**Governance Documentation (Clause 7.5, A.5.1, A.5.13).** Comprehensive and controlled documented information is fundamental to an ISO 27001 ISMS, including policies and procedures.

**Executive Support (Clause 5.1).** Top management must demonstrate leadership and commitment to the ISMS, including providing adequate resources.

**Continuous Improvement (Clause 10.1, A.5.16, A.5.26).** The ISMS must be continually improved based on performance monitoring, incident learning, and changing circumstances.

**Targeted Communication (Clause 7.3, A.6.3).** Personnel must be aware of the information security policy, their contribution to the ISMS, and implications of non-conformance.

**Cultural Relevance (Clause 7.3, A.6.3).** Awareness efforts should be designed to resonate with the organization's culture and diverse workforce for maximum effectiveness.

**Active Participation (Clause 7.3, A.5.15).** Personnel should understand their role in incident reporting and actively contribute to information security objectives.

**Feedback Responsiveness (Clause 10.1, A.5.16).** Mechanisms for collecting and acting on feedback are crucial for ISMS continual improvement and incident learning.

**Performance Measurement (Clause 9.1, A.5.25).** The performance and effectiveness of the ISMS, including awareness programs, must be monitored, measured, and evaluated.

**Risk Driven Assessment (Clause 6.1.2, A.5.22).** Risk assessments drive the selection of controls and inform the content and focus of awareness and training programs.

**Behavioral Impact (Clause 9.1, A.6.3).** Evaluating the effectiveness of awareness and training should include assessing changes in employee security behavior.

**Relevant Tailored Training (Clause 7.2, A.6.3).** Training content must be appropriate for the roles and responsibilities of personnel, based on identified competence needs.

**Effective Learning Methods (Clause 7.2, A.6.3).** Training methods should be effective in achieving the desired competence and awareness levels for information security.

**Accessible Inclusive Training (Clause 7.2, A.6.3).** Training and awareness materials must be accessible and provided to all relevant personnel and interested parties.

**Flexible Effective Delivery (Clause 7.2, A.6.3).** Delivery methods should be flexible to ensure effective learning and retention across the diverse organizational workforce.

**Integrated Training Lifecycle (Clause 7.2, A.6.3).** Training and awareness should be integrated into the employee lifecycle, from onboarding through ongoing competence development.
